Landsat active fire data are more than 1,000 times more resolute than MODIS and more than 150 times more resolute than VIIRS. The algorithm routinely detects fires as small as a few square meters or smaller, providing spatially explicit detail of the location and extent of fire activity at the time of a satellite overpass. Active fire fronts and scattered smaller fires within the fire perimeter are routinely detected.

However, these relatively high spatial resolution data are provided at a lower temporal resolution compared to the daily observation frequency afforded by MODIS and VIIRS. Between both Landsat OLI instruments, areas in the lower to mid latitudes are imaged every 8 days. The temporal resolution of Landsat data can be increased though the collection of nighttime Landsat 8 and Landsat 9 imagery for targeted areas.

The following graphic shows Landsat active fire data compared for MODIS (Terra and Aqua) (1km) and VIIRS (Suomi NPP and NOAA-20) (375m) for the Double Creek Fire in Northeast Oregon on September 10, 2022.
