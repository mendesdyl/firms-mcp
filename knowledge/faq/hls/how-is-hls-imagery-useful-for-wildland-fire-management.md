HLS time-series data lends itself to land surface applications at the field/plot scale, which strongly supports pre- and post-fire monitoring, agricultural health, and natural hazard impacts.

The HLS short-wave infrared false-color composite imagery layer is particularly useful for identifying and monitoring burned areas. It complements the false-color composite MODIS and VIIRS imagery by providing higher fidelity imagery to view active fire fronts, discern fire impacts and damage on the landscape and, to a qualitative degree, how much those areas have recovered.

HLS false-color composite imagery helps better delineate burned areas using short-wave and near-infrared wavelengths undetectable by the human eye. The result is high-resolution imagery where burned areas appear almost brick red, making them much easier to see on the landscape.

Additionally, HLS imagery and its derived vegetation indices are highly useful for assessing pre-fire conditions, evaluating active fire risks, and mapping post-fire impacts. Here is how each specific index is utilized:

- NDVI (Vegetation Greenness): Historically used to measure and assess vegetation greenness and health of vegetation which may be affected by drought stress. NDVI can be used to map the extent of burned areas and assess fire impacts on aboveground vegetation.
- NDMI (Moisture Content): Highly sensitive to liquid water content in vegetation. It provides critical information on the moisture content of fuels, helping managers understand vegetation drought stress and susceptibility to increased fire risk.
- NBR (Burn Severity): Also sensitive to vegetation water content as well as ash and exposed, dry soils, it is used to delineate burned areas and measure the degree of fire effects (burn severity). Comparing NBR data before and after a fire helps map the burned extent, vegetation impacts and supports post-fire response and recovery planning.
- NBR2 (Water Sensitivity & Post-fire Conditions): A variant of NBR that highlights water sensitivity. It is excellent for assessing post-fire vegetation conditions and identifying small variations in moisture content and recovery stages. NBR2 is particularly useful for delineating burned areas in biomes where the burn signal is subtle, such as grass/shrublands, coastal marshes, or herbaceous wetlands.
