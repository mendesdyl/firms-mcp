Geostationary satellite sensors view the same area of Earth’s surface at all times, while polar-orbiting sensors, such as MODIS and VIIRS, typically view the same area of Earth’s surface twice daily. Geostationary satellite sensors can provide repeated observations at 10 to 15 minute intervals, making it possible to capture their growth and change of fires and to detect fires that may not be detected at longer temporal intervals. However, the spatial resolution of geostationary satellite data is generally coarser and therefore less sensitive to small fires. The exception is the new Meteosat Third Generation satellite, which provides active fire detections with a 1 km pixel resolution at sub-nadir. As with other geostationary satellites, the resolution of the pixel grows coarser away from sub-nadir.
